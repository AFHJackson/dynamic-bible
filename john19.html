To the end; It is finished

“To the end” (13:1, eis telos) and “It is finished” (19:30, tetelestai) are two forms of the same Greek root, tel-, “end, completion”: telos is the noun and teleō, “bring to its end, finish”, is the verb. Telos can also mean “to the uttermost, completely”. John opens the Book of Glory saying that Jesus ēgapēsen his own “to the end” (13:1), and Jesus’ last word on the cross is “It is finished” (19:30; 19:28 has the root twice, “everything was already finished … so that the Scripture would be finished”). The two verses mark the beginning and the end of the same love.

Handed over the pneuma

John writes paredōken to pneuma, “handed over the pneuma”. At its plainest it means that Jesus died: pneuma is breath as well as spirit, and he gave up his breath. But “hand over” is John’s word for Jesus being handed over by Judas, the authorities and Pilate (18:2, 18:30, 19:16), and no other Gospel uses it for Jesus’ death. Because pneuma is a held word, you can see that it is the same word as the pneuma promised in 7:39 and given in 20:22 (“Receive holy pneuma”). Raymond Brown and others read a second sense here as well: the Spirit given from the cross. This is debated; many scholars see only his death here, and the giving of the Spirit in 20:22.
