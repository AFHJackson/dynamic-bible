A passage in brackets

The story of the woman caught in adultery (7:53-8:11) is not in the earliest manuscripts of John, and some manuscripts place it elsewhere (later in John, or in Luke). Our Greek text (SBLGNT) prints it here inside double brackets, and most English Bibles print it here in brackets with a note, as this translation does. Two readings in 8:11 also differ from most English Bibles. Our Greek has krinō, so Jesus says “Neither do I judge you”, the verb of 8:15 (“I judge no one”); most Bibles follow manuscripts with katakrinō, “Neither do I condemn you”, the verb of the question in 8:10 (“Has no one condemned you?”). And our Greek has no “from now on”: Jesus says “Go, and no longer hamartane”, where most Bibles say “from now on” (apo tou nyn, in other manuscripts).

“I am”

Jesus does not say “I was” but “I am”, and he sets it against Abraham’s “came into being”, the same contrast as the prologue, where the logos “was” and everything “came into being” (1:1-3). The words point to Isaiah 43:10, where God says in the Greek Old Testament “so that you may know … that I am” (egō eimi), and to Exodus 3:14, where God names himself at the burning bush (Greek “I am the one who is”; Hebrew “I am who I am”).
