Paul and Timothy, douloi of Christ Jesus, to all the holy people in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, along with the overseers and diakonois : Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

I eucharistō my God whenever I remember you, always, in every deēsei of mine for all of you, making my deēsin with joy, because of your sharing in the gospel from the first day until now. I am confident of this very thing: that the one who began an agathon work in you will bring it to completion by the day of Christ Jesus. It is right for me to think this about all of you, since I have you in my heart, with all of you being sharers with me in grace, both in my chains and in the defense and confirmation of the gospel. For God is my witness how I long for all of you with the affection of Christ Jesus. And this I proseuchomai : that your agapē may abound still more and more in knowledge and every kind of discernment, so that you can assess what is excellent, so that you may be sincere and free from stumbling for the day of Christ, filled with the fruit of righteousness that comes through Jesus Christ, for God’s glory and praise.

But I want you to ginōskein , brothers and sisters, that what has happened to me has actually led to the progress of the gospel, so that my chains have become known as being for Christ throughout the governor’s headquarters and to everyone else, and most of the brothers and sisters, confident in the Lord because of my chains, dare all the more to speak the logon without fear.

Some indeed proclaim Christ even out of envy and conflict, while others also do so out of good will. The latter do so out of agapēs , eidotes that I am positioned to defend the gospel. The others, out of rivalry, announce Christ with impure motives, thinking they can stir up distress in my imprisonment. What, then? Only that in every way, whether as a pretext or in truth, Christ is proclaimed, and in this I rejoice. And I will also rejoice, for I oida that this will turn out for my deliverance through your deēseōs and the supply of the pneumatos of Jesus Christ, in keeping with my eager expectation and hope that I will be ashamed in nothing, but with complete boldness, now as always, Christ will be magnified in my body, whether through zōēs or through death. For me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain. But if to live in flesh means this for me: fruit from work— then what I will choose, I do not know. But I am pressed by the two, having the desire to depart and be with Christ, for that is much better still, but to remain in the flesh is more necessary for your sake. And being confident of this, I oida that I will remain and stay with all of you for your progress and joy in pisteōs so that your boast may abound in Christ Jesus through me, through my presence with you again.

Only live as citizens in a way worthy of the gospel of Christ, so that whether I come and see you or am away, I may hear about you, that you stand firm in one pneumati , with one psychē contending together for the pistei of the gospel, and not being frightened in any way by those who oppose you. This is a sign to them of destruction, but of your rescue, and this comes from God, because you have been granted on Christ’s behalf not only to pisteuein in him, but also to suffer on his behalf, having the same struggle that you saw in me and now hear in me.

Old Testament echo

The words “this will turn out for my deliverance” repeat, word for word, Job 13:16 in the Greek Old Testament (the Septuagint). There Job, accused by his friends, is sure that God will vindicate him. Paul, in chains and facing a verdict, speaks Job's words. The deliverance he expects may be release, vindication before God, or both; scholars weigh these. The same Greek word, sōtēria, is rendered by its context in 1:28 and 2:12.
